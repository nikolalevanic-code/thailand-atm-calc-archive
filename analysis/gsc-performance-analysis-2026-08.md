# Thailand ATM Calculator — Search Performance Analysis

**Reporting window:** 3–9 August 2026 (seven days)  
**Comparison baseline:** 22 March–27 May 2026 (67 days)  
**Data source:** User-provided Google Search Console exports

## Intent

This review assesses whether the site is gaining qualified organic-search traction, what the recent data implies about the work completed in May, and which small set of actions is most likely to improve traffic quality and monetisation readiness. The objective is not to treat a seven-day snapshot as proof of a trend; it is to use it as a directional signal and establish the next measurable experiments.

## Executive assessment

>The site is no longer merely being indexed: it is now receiving roughly **50 Google clicks per week**, or an annualised run rate of about **217 organic clicks per month** if this seven-day pace held. Daily impressions are **8.7×** the March–May baseline and daily clicks are **12.9×** higher. Average ranking improved modestly from approximately position **7.0 to 6.5**, while CTR moved from **0.21% to 0.32%**.

>This is encouraging but not yet a reliable monetisation base. The positive conclusion is that the SEO/content changes appear consistent with much stronger discovery and click volume. The caution is that the latest extract covers only one week, and Google Search Console does not show visitor intent, calculator completion, affiliate CTA clicks, or revenue. It therefore cannot prove the cause of growth or quantify commercial performance.

## The most important search trend: AEON intent

The query data is heavily concentrated around **AEON as the lower-fee ATM alternative**. Across 106 AEON-related queries, the site earned 637 impressions at an average position of 5.0 but only one click, a 0.16% CTR. Multiple specific variants rank between positions 2 and 4, including searches for “AEON Thailand ATM foreign card fee 150 baht 2026,” yet generate no clicks.

This is the clearest signal for the next content asset. Searchers want a precise, practical answer: whether AEON still charges 150 THB, which foreign cards are affected, how to find the machines, and whether the apparently lower fee outweighs convenience. The existing “best ATM” page is succeeding broadly, but it is being asked to answer a large cluster of more specific AEON/location questions.

## Traffic quality matters more than headline impressions

The geography and device reports indicate that many current impressions are unlikely to be equally valuable. The United States produced **43.5% of impressions but only 4.0% of clicks**, with a 0.03% CTR. Thailand produced **29.2% of impressions and 38.0% of clicks**, despite its lower impression volume. This may indicate that people already in Thailand, or travellers using Thai networks, have more immediate need. It is a hypothesis rather than a fact: Search Console country data identifies the searcher’s location, not whether they are a tourist, resident, or planning a trip.

The mobile pattern is more compelling. Mobile generated **25.3% of impressions but 80.0% of clicks**, with a 1.01% CTR compared with desktop’s 0.09%. The site’s mobile-first calculator is therefore aligned with the more engaged segment. Future articles and CTAs should prioritise quick, decisive answers on mobile rather than long introductions or generic travel advice.

A 0.50% CTR is a sensible next 60–90 day operating target. It requires improving snippet relevance and page usefulness, not simply adding more pages. The larger 1.00%+ scenarios should be treated as longer-term outcomes because a large share of current impressions comes from broad, low-click desktop searches.

## Technical SEO correction: canonical URLs need attention

>The current application source declares one static canonical URL — `https://www.thailand-atm-calculator.com` — in `client/index.html`. The blog layout updates the browser title and meta description after JavaScript loads, but it does not update the canonical link for each article route.

That configuration means the blog pages can emit a homepage canonical rather than a self-referencing canonical. Google may choose to ignore a conflicting canonical signal, and the pages are evidently appearing separately in Search Console, but it is still an avoidable technical risk. It should be corrected before expanding the content cluster further.

The public assets directory also contains no `sitemap.xml` or `robots.txt`. A sitemap is especially useful now that the site has eight public indexable URLs. It is a small, low-risk discovery improvement; a robots file should reference the sitemap and permit crawling of public content.

## Important correction to the previous SEO plan

Two earlier recommendations should be reframed based on Google’s current guidance. Google removed the FAQ rich-result feature from Search in May 2026, so **FAQPage schema should not be expected to increase visible search-result real estate** for this travel site. The on-page FAQs are still useful for readers and for answering long-tail questions, but they are not a rich-result growth tactic anymore. [1]

Similarly, Google clarified that `llms.txt` has **no positive or negative effect on Google Search visibility or rankings**. Keeping it is harmless if other systems use it, but it should not consume SEO effort. [1]

Article structured data remains a supported type for article-related rich-result features, though eligibility does not guarantee a special presentation. Treat it as clean content metadata, not a lever that replaces strong content, links, and query relevance. [2]

## Revenue readiness

At the present weekly pace, the site is on a run rate of roughly **217 Google organic clicks per month**. Under the earlier working model of 1,500–2,000 monthly visitors for approximately $500/month in affiliate revenue, this is still a **6.9×–9.2× traffic gap**. That model should now be replaced with observed funnel data once affiliate links are live: actual click-through rate, completed application rate, approval rate, and realised commission.

Display ads are unlikely to be meaningful at this traffic level and would introduce UX and trust costs on the calculator. The more rational near-term goal is to make affiliate attribution measurable, then grow qualified calculator users. The `Wise vs Revolut` and `no-fee withdrawal` pages are the appropriate locations for clearly labelled, non-deceptive affiliate calls to action once links are approved.

## What not to do yet

Avoid adding display ads, launching many loosely related articles, or creating AI-generated location lists without primary verification. The data shows that one tightly aligned problem — choosing the lowest-cost ATM — is working. The next move is to deepen that topical authority and make the resulting traffic measurable, not broaden the site into generic Thailand travel content.

## Measurement cadence

Use a **28-day vs. previous 28-day** Search Console comparison rather than a seven-day snapshot for decisions. Monitor total clicks, impressions, CTR, average position, and the three lead pages individually. In analytics, track calculator completion and, later, affiliate outcomes. A simple monthly review should answer: which page brought visitors, did they use the calculator, and did they reach a commercial CTA?
